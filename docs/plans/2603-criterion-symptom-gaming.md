### #2906 — The audit substrate reports a lock held by a sibling worker as corruption, escalating the story

- selected diagnose run `cf417b6f46da` (`body_contains_criterion`)
- landed commit: `57fa575e088257361e20fc50c708b3240ba10838`
- classification: `cause_addressing`

Recorded fix-success criterion:

> Given N sprint workers resuming concurrently against the same audit
> substrate (reproducing contention, e.g. via a held write lock or a
> deliberately short busy_timeout in a test), a worker that encounters
> "database is locked" must not raise SubstrateCorruptError and must not
> reach the operator as an ESCALATE routed to `forge audits rebuild`. Either
> the open retries/waits up to a bound appropriate to the operation (not a
> fixed ceiling that a larger store or higher --parallel silently exceeds) and
> succeeds once the sibling releases the lock, or — if it still times out — the
> resulting failure is classified and surfaced to the operator as transient
> lock contention (or existing shared-infrastructure attribution), distinct
> from `SubstrateCorruptError` and distinct from `unknown_needs_rca`, and does
> NOT recommend `forge audits rebuild`. A file that genuinely fails
> `PRAGMA integrity_check` or genuinely cannot be opened for a non-lock reason
> must still raise SubstrateCorruptError as today.

Change that would satisfy it while leaving the cause in place: Wrap `_open_validated` in a retry loop that retries on any `sqlite3.DatabaseError`. A locked substrate then succeeds once the sibling releases it, the operator never sees the rebuild ESCALATE, and a genuinely corrupt file still fails after the retries — every clause satisfied while `OperationalError` and real corruption stay conflated in one `except`, which is the cause.

Notes: Landed change introduces `SubstrateLockTimeoutError` as a class distinct from `SubstrateCorruptError`, with a size-derived bounded wait rather than a fixed ceiling. The conflation is removed at its source.
